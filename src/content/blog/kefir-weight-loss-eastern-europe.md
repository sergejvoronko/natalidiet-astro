Long before "gut health" became a trending hashtag, kefir was an everyday drink across Eastern Europe: a jar of tangy, slightly fizzy fermented milk on the kitchen counter, drunk with breakfast, poured over potatoes or turned into a cold summer soup.

Today it is sold in health food shops as a probiotic superstar. The real story is more modest and more useful. Kefir will not melt weight off on its own, but it is a filling, protein-rich, low-calorie food that makes a lighter way of eating easier to stick to.

### What is kefir, really? (And why it's not just drinkable yogurt)

Both kefir and yogurt are fermented milk, but they are made differently.

Yogurt is fermented with a small number of bacterial strains. Kefir is fermented with "kefir grains", which are not grains at all: they are a living culture of many different bacteria and yeasts held together in a jelly-like matrix. That mix gives kefir its sour tang, its light fizz and a wider range of microbes than most yogurts.

Fermentation also uses up part of the lactose in the milk. Some people with lactose intolerance find they tolerate kefir better than plain milk, though it is not lactose-free, so start with a small glass if that applies to you.

### Kefir and weight: what it can and cannot do

Research on kefir and weight loss is still limited, and the results are mixed. Some small studies have found benefits, others have found no difference, and there is no good evidence that kefir "boosts metabolism" or burns fat. What it does offer is practical:

#### 1. It is filling for few calories

A glass of plain low-fat kefir is low in calories and gives you a useful amount of protein. Protein helps you feel full, so kefir works well as a snack or as part of a light breakfast in place of something sweeter.

#### 2. It can replace richer ingredients

Kefir's tang means it can stand in for sour cream or mayonnaise in dressings, dips and cold soups. That swap alone takes a lot of calories out of many traditional dishes without losing the flavour.

#### 3. It supports a varied gut microbiome

Fermented foods like kefir add live microbes to your diet, and a diet rich in fermented foods and fibre is associated with a more diverse gut microbiome. How much this matters for weight is still being researched, so treat it as a bonus, not the main reason to drink kefir.

### Practical ways to use kefir every day

The easiest way to benefit is to make kefir a small, regular part of your meals.

#### A breakfast smoothie
A kefir smoothie is filling without being heavy. A simple version:
* 1 cup plain kefir
* 1/2 cup frozen mixed berries (like raspberries and blueberries)
* 1 tablespoon of ground flaxseed
* A small handful of spinach (you won't taste it, I promise)

It gives you protein, some healthy fat and fibre, and keeps you going until lunch.

#### A base for savoury dips and dressings
Kefir makes a good substitute for sour cream or mayonnaise in cold sauces. A simple cucumber salad dressing:
* Whisk 1/2 cup of kefir with a crushed garlic clove, a tablespoon of freshly chopped dill, a pinch of salt, and a squeeze of lemon juice.
* Pour it over thinly sliced cucumbers. It's a perfect, refreshing side for grilled chicken or fish.

#### An afternoon snack
A small glass (about 150ml) of plain kefir in the afternoon is a filling alternative to biscuits or a sugary coffee drink, and it takes the edge off your appetite before dinner.

#### In cold soups
In summer, kefir is the base of *chłodnik*, the Polish cold beet soup. It is bright pink, refreshing and light, a good example of how traditional dishes already use this ingredient well.

### Shop-bought or homemade?

Plain shop-bought kefir is a perfectly good choice. Check the label: plain kefir usually has no added sugar, while flavoured versions often do.

If you want to make your own, you need active kefir grains, which you can get from a friend or buy online.

1. **Place** about one tablespoon of kefir grains in a clean glass jar.
2. **Add** about 2 cups of whole milk.
3. **Cover** the jar with a coffee filter or cheesecloth and secure it with a rubber band. This lets it breathe but keeps dust out.
4. **Leave** it at room temperature until it has thickened slightly and smells pleasantly sour, usually about a day. The warmer the room, the faster it ferments.
5. **Strain** the kefir through a fine plastic strainer into a new jar and keep it in the fridge.
6. **Put** the grains back into the first jar, add more milk, and start again.

It takes a couple of minutes a day, and the grains grow over time, so you will soon have some to share.

Kefir is not a miracle food. It is an old, simple one that fits naturally into a lighter, more traditional way of eating, and that is reason enough to keep a bottle in the fridge.
