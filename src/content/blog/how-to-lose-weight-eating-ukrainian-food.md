It is a common question: from Ukrainians living abroad who miss their food, from people who grew up with these dishes and want to keep cooking them, and from anyone who has tasted borscht and thought: surely this cannot be diet food.

It can. Traditional Ukrainian everyday cooking was built on many of the things nutritionists recommend: fermented foods, plenty of high-fibre vegetables, slow broths, not much processing. The weight problem usually comes from what got added later, not from the tradition itself.

## What actually drives the weight gain
So let us be honest about the culprits before we defend the rest. It is rarely the borscht. It is lard as the default cooking fat, salo eaten daily rather than as an occasional slice, four or five thick slices of white bread at every meal, sour cream ladled onto everything, deep-fried varenyky and deruny, and sugar in the tea and the syrniki. Fix those six habits and most of the tradition can stay exactly as it is.

## The dishes that are already diet food
The list is longer than most people expect.

### Borscht
A large bowl made without lard runs about 150–200 calories. Beets bring folate, cabbage brings fibre and vitamin C, and the broth stays lean. It is filling, cheap and nourishing.

### Green borshch (sorrel soup)
About 150 calories a bowl, around 230 with the traditional hard-boiled egg, and a good source of vitamin C from the sorrel. The egg adds the protein. A weight-loss meal that happens to be a cultural staple.

### Buckwheat kasha (hrechana kasha)
Ukraine's everyday grain, and one of the better things you can put on a plate: a gentle effect on blood sugar compared with white rice, plenty of magnesium and fibre, and useful plant protein. Use it as your side at every meal in place of white bread or white rice.

### Varenyky with potato and cottage cheese (baked, not fried)
Bake them instead of frying and the dish turns diet-friendly without losing the point. Five baked varenyky come in at about 300 calories: a satisfying meal with familiar flavours.

### Holubtsi in tomato sauce
Swap the sour cream sauce for the tomato-braised version and use lean mince, and two holubtsi land near 280 calories and still fill you up.

### Kefir
Plain kefir is the most underrated food in the whole tradition. 200ml of the low-fat kind is about 80–100 calories and 7g of protein, plus live cultures. It is filling for very few calories, which makes it a useful daily habit, even though research on kefir and weight specifically is still limited.

### Vinegret salad
The beet, potato, carrot and pickle salad earns its place on any table. Naturally fermented, uncooked pickles bring live cultures, the beets bring colour and folate, and dressed with a little olive oil and herbs it stays modest in calories.

## A daily framework
### Morning: the kefir habit
Start the day with 200ml plain kefir: filling, protein-rich and low in calories. Follow it with buckwheat porridge cooked in water, a few berries or a small apple on top. About 350–400 calories.

### Lunch: soup first, always
The Ukrainian midday meal starts with soup, and in this framework that part is non-negotiable. It is the rule that makes the rest work. A bowl of borscht or green borshch before the main course quietly cuts how much you eat at that meal, and it never feels like restriction. Then a modest portion of the main dish, a side of sauerkraut, a dill cucumber salad. About 450–500 calories.

### Afternoon: the snack
Cottage cheese (tvaroh) with fresh dill and cucumber, or a small bowl of kefir with a handful of walnuts. About 150–180 calories. Both are ordinary Ukrainian foods that happen to make excellent diet snacks.

### Dinner: lighter than lunch
Traditionally, midday was the big meal and dinner the smaller one. Modern schedules often flip that; whether timing itself matters is still debated, but a lighter dinner is an easy way to keep the daily total down. Keep the evening lighter: baked fish with roasted vegetables, a light egg soup, or buckwheat with mushrooms. About 350–400 calories.

## Adapting specific recipes
### Deruny (potato pancakes)
Grate the potatoes and squeeze out all the liquid. That is the step most home cooks skip, and it is the one that matters. Mix with an egg, low-fat cottage cheese and dill, then bake at 220°C on a lightly sprayed tray, about 15 minutes, then 10–12 minutes after flipping. You get crisp, golden deruny at about 200 calories for six small ones, far less than the fried version.

### Syrniki (cottage cheese pancakes)
Already one of the better traditional breakfasts, since it is mostly cottage cheese and egg. To lighten it: low-fat cottage cheese, oat flour instead of white, a non-stick pan with just an oil spray. Serve with fresh berries rather than jam or sour cream. About 150 calories for three, before toppings.

### Olivier salad
Replace full-fat mayonnaise with a 50/50 mix of low-fat Greek yogurt and a teaspoon of Dijon mustard. Add more vegetables (cucumber, peas, carrots) and reduce the potato volume. Use chicken breast instead of fatty ham. The result is lighter and a little tangier than the original, at roughly half the calories.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I eat salo (cured pork fat) on a diet?

Yes, occasionally. A small slice (20–30g) of salo as an occasional treat will not derail a diet. What causes problems is eating salo every day in large quantities. The Ukrainian tradition of a thin slice of salo on dark rye bread with garlic and a pickle is, in moderation, a culturally meaningful and not excessive indulgence.

### Is Ukrainian bread healthy?

Traditional Ukrainian sourdough rye bread is nutritious, high in fibre, lower GI than wheat bread, fermented with a sourdough starter. One or two slices per day is completely compatible with weight loss. The problem is eating 4–5 thick slices at every meal. Portion it thoughtfully.

### How do I handle family meals where I cannot control the food?

This is a real and emotionally complex challenge, declining food in Eastern European cultures can feel like rejecting love. One approach: eat a smaller portion of everything with genuine appreciation. Ask for the recipe so you can make a lighter version at home. Bring a dish you have made healthily to share. Frame it as honouring the tradition by cooking it more often yourself, not as rejecting the food.

### What is the most weight-loss-friendly traditional Ukrainian meal?

Green borshch with a hard-boiled egg, about 230 calories, is one of the most diet-friendly traditional meals. A close second: buckwheat kasha with braised mushrooms and a side of sauerkraut, about 300 calories and about as nourishing as cheap food gets.
