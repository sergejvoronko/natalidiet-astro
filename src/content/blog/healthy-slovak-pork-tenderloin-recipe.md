Pork is the backbone of Slovak and Central European cuisine. Traditional Slovak pork dishes are often quite fatty, braised pork shoulder, roasted pork knuckle, fatty pork ribs. Delicious, but heavy. However, there is one cut of pork that is lean and naturally diet-friendly: the tenderloin.
Pork tenderloin is one of the leanest proteins you can buy, about as lean as chicken breast, with excellent flavour when properly cooked. This recipe uses the caraway and garlic that season traditional Slovak roast pork, plus mustard, a combination that is intensely aromatic and very satisfying. At about 380 calories and 55 grams of protein per serving, it is the highest-protein dish in this series.

## Ingredients
Serves 4.

### For the pork
- 2 pork tenderloins (approx. 500g each)
- 2 tsp caraway seeds, lightly crushed in a mortar
- 4 cloves garlic, minced or pressed
- 2 tbsp whole-grain Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp sweet paprika
- 1 tsp dried marjoram
- 1 tsp olive oil
- Juice of 1/2 lemon
- Salt and black pepper

### For the roasted vegetables (same pan)
- 300g cherry tomatoes
- 2 medium courgettes (zucchini), sliced into half-moons
- 1 red onion, cut into wedges
- 1 red bell pepper, chunked
- 1 tsp olive oil
- Fresh thyme sprigs

## Instructions
### Marinate (1 hour minimum, overnight is ideal)
- Combine crushed caraway seeds, garlic, mustard, both paprikas, marjoram, olive oil, lemon juice, salt, and pepper in a small bowl. Mix into a paste.
- Score the pork tenderloin lightly with a knife (3–4 shallow cuts) to help the marinade penetrate. Rub the marinade all over the pork. Place in a zip-lock bag or covered dish. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour, ideally overnight.

### Cook
- Take the pork out of the fridge 20 minutes before cooking so it cooks more evenly.
- Preheat oven to 200°C / 400°F. Toss the vegetables with olive oil, salt, and thyme sprigs. Spread across a large roasting tin.
- Heat a heavy oven-safe skillet or grill pan over high heat. Sear the pork tenderloins for 2 minutes per side (all four sides) until nicely browned, about 8 minutes total. This creates a flavour crust.
- Place seared pork on TOP of the vegetables in the roasting tin. Roast in the preheated oven for 18–22 minutes.
- Check the internal temperature with a meat probe: 63°C / 145°F, followed by a 3-minute rest, is the USDA safe minimum for whole cuts and gives a slightly pink, juicy result. 71°C / 160°F is fully cooked through (slightly firmer).
- Remove from oven. REST for 10 minutes before slicing, this keeps the pork juicy. The temperature will rise a couple of degrees during resting.
- Slice on the diagonal into medallions about 1.5cm thick. Serve over the roasted vegetables with pan juices drizzled over.

## Nutrition facts
Per serving (serves 4, pork with roasted vegetables): about 380 kcal, 55g protein, 12g fat, 12g carbohydrate. Values are estimates calculated from the ingredients.

- Mushroom jus: Deglaze the pan with 100ml white wine + 100ml broth + sautéed mushrooms, reduce to a sauce (adds ~40 kcal per serving)

## Frequently asked questions

### Is pork tenderloin leaner than chicken breast?

They are very close. Cooked, 100g of pork tenderloin has about 143 kcal and 3.5g fat, while 100g of cooked skinless chicken breast has about 165 kcal and 3.6g fat. Pork tenderloin is an underrated lean protein.

### Can I cook this in a slow cooker?

Pork tenderloin is not ideal for slow cookers, it is so lean that it dries out with long cooking. For slow cookers, choose pork shoulder or leg instead, which are fattier and tolerate longer cooking. The oven method is best for tenderloin.

### What traditional Slovak dishes does this recipe draw from?

The caraway and garlic seasoning comes from traditional Slovak roast pork, and mustard is a classic Central European partner for pork. Roasted vegetables replace the usual potatoes or dumplings to keep it lighter. Traditional Slovak flavour in a modern, lighter format.

### How do I know when pork tenderloin is cooked?

A meat thermometer is by far the most reliable method. The USDA considers 63°C / 145°F with a 3-minute rest safe for whole pork cuts, and at this temperature the meat will have a slight pink blush, which is normal. Cooking to 71°C / 160°F produces fully white, slightly firmer meat. Much beyond that and tenderloin quickly dries out.
