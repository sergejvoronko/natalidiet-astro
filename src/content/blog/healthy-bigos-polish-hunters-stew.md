Bigos is Poland's national dish, a slow-cooked stew of sauerkraut, fresh cabbage, mixed meats, dried mushrooms, and red wine. A layered dish, and a slow one.
The health challenge with traditional bigos is the fatty smoked meats. This version replaces them with lean pork tenderloin and lean turkey kielbasa, uses plenty of sauerkraut and fresh cabbage for volume and fibre, and still produces the deep, smoke-and-acid flavour that makes bigos one of Eastern Europe's great comfort foods.

## Ingredients
Serves 5.

- 400g naturally-fermented sauerkraut (from a jar, not vinegar-brined can)
- 300g fresh white cabbage, finely shredded
- 300g lean pork tenderloin, cut into 2cm cubes
- 200g lean turkey kielbasa, sliced
- 20g dried porcini mushrooms, soaked in 200ml warm water
- 200g fresh cremini mushrooms, sliced
- 1 large onion diced, 3 cloves garlic minced
- 150ml dry red wine (or extra broth + 1 tbsp balsamic)
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 bay leaves, 4 allspice berries, 1 tsp caraway seeds, 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp olive oil, salt and black pepper

## Instructions
- Soak porcini 20 min. Drain, reserve soaking liquid through a sieve. Chop roughly.
- Sear pork tenderloin cubes in olive oil over high heat, 2–3 min. Remove and set aside.
- Sauté onion 6 min until golden. Add garlic, caraway, allspice, paprika, cook 1 min.
- Add turkey kielbasa 2 min. Add tomato paste 1 min. Add red wine, let reduce 2 min.
- Add sauerkraut, fresh cabbage, both mushroom types and their soaking liquid. Add bay leaves and ~200ml water to just cover.
- Return seared pork. Stir. Bring to gentle simmer. Cover, cook VERY LOW heat for 90 minutes.
- Remove bay leaves. Season. Serve with dark rye bread, or eat as is for lower carbs.

## Nutrition facts
Per serving (serves 5, without rye bread): about 250 kcal, 23g protein, 7g fat, 14g carbohydrate, 5g fibre. Values are estimates calculated from the ingredients.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is bigos healthy?

Fundamentally yes, it is mostly fermented and fresh cabbage with modest meat, seasoned with mushrooms and spices. With lean substitutions, bigos becomes a good diet food: high in protein, a source of fibre, and very filling for its calories. The long simmer does kill the live cultures in the sauerkraut, so for probiotics eat some raw sauerkraut on the side.

### Can I make vegetarian bigos?

Yes, omit meat, use smoked paprika and extra dried mushrooms for depth. Add 400g cooked white beans or chickpeas for protein. Meatless mushroom bigos is traditional in some Polish homes on Christmas Eve.

### What wine for bigos?

Any dry red works, a simple Merlot or Cabernet is fine. Some cooks add a splash of Madeira instead. Without alcohol: 150ml extra broth + 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar.
