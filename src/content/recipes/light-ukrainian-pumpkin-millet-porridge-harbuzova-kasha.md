  - q: Why does my millet porridge sometimes taste bitter?

Harbuzova kasha is a cornerstone of autumn cooking in Ukraine. Historically prepared slowly in a traditional clay oven called a pech, this pumpkin and millet porridge is sweet, creamy, and comforting. Millet remains a staple grain across Eastern Europe, and when paired with fresh pumpkin, it creates a naturally sweet base that requires very little added sugar. The golden grains swell in the simmering liquid, filling the kitchen with the warm, earthy aroma of cinnamon and sweet squash as it thickens on the stove.

I prefer making this lighter version using low-fat milk and just a modest spoonful of honey. This keeps the dish fit for a balanced diet, offering a breakfast rich in complex carbohydrates and dietary fibre. The pumpkin breaks down as it cooks, naturally thickening the liquid and giving the porridge a vibrant orange hue. Using butternut squash or a small sugar pumpkin provides the best natural flavour without needing extra sweeteners.

For a varied morning routine, I often alternate this dish with a [Golden Buckwheat Porridge with Berries & Honey](/recipes/golden-buckwheat-porridge-berries-honey/). When serving the pumpkin porridge, a sprinkle of toasted pumpkin seeds on top adds a welcome crunch against the soft, creamy grains. Fresh berries or a few apple slices on the side also complement the autumn flavours perfectly. 

Leftovers store wonderfully in an airtight container in the fridge for a few days. Because the starch in the millet continues to absorb liquid, the porridge becomes quite dense overnight. The trick is to add a splash of water or milk to the saucepan and stir vigorously over a low flame to bring back that freshly cooked, creamy texture. If you want to experiment, you can easily swap the dairy milk for an unsweetened almond or oat alternative.
