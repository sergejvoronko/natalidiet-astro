Segedínský guláš is a beloved staple across Czech and Slovak kitchens, despite tracing its origins back to the Hungarian Székelygulyás. It brings together two pillars of Central European cooking: savoury meat and sharp fermented cabbage. The sound of diced pork searing in the pot and the earthy smell of sweet paprika blooming in warm oil instantly make any kitchen feel inviting. 

Classic versions of this stew rely heavily on fatty cuts of pork belly or rich shoulder, finishing with generous pours of heavy cream. I prefer using a leaner cut of pork, such as the leg, to keep the protein high while cutting down on saturated fat. Thickening the vibrant red sauce with tempered low-fat yogurt instead of cream maintains that signature silky texture without the heaviness. 

Fermented foods are a brilliant addition to everyday meals. The sauerkraut provides a bright, acidic bite that cuts right through the richness of the paprika base, while also adding plenty of dietary fibre. It is a hearty, warming bowl that feels substantial but sits lightly.

## Serving Ideas

For a complete meal, boiled potatoes are a great, straightforward side that quickly soak up the thick sauce. You can also serve this stew with a thick slice of dark rye bread to wipe the bowl clean, which is how it often appears in local pubs.

## Storage and Variations

Stews with an acidic base almost always taste better on the second day. The flavours get a chance to mingle, deepening the savoury notes of the paprika and caraway over time. It keeps very well in an airtight container in the fridge for up to three days. When reheating, I just warm it gently on the stove over low heat so the yogurt does not split. If you prefer a smokier profile, you can swap half a tablespoon of the sweet paprika for smoked paprika.
