The smell of simmering dill and earthy mushrooms always transports me straight to the damp, mossy forests of Central Europe. Kulajda is a traditional dish that normally leans heavily on thick double cream and heaps of butter. My mum made this every autumn when the wild mushrooms were in full season, drying whatever we could not eat fresh. I remember standing by the stove, watching the opaque white broth bubble around chunks of golden potato, waiting for the sharp tang of vinegar to hit the air. 

I wanted to keep that comforting, velvety texture without making it a heavy meal. This healthy wild mushroom soup is my favourite way to enjoy low calorie Czech food without feeling deprived. By using semi-skimmed milk whisked with a tiny bit of flour, you get that same luxurious mouthfeel. The dried mushrooms do the hard work here, infusing the broth with an intense, woody flavour that pairs perfectly with the bright notes of fresh dill. 

This lighter Czech Kulajda recipe fits right into a balanced diet, offering a generous dose of fibre from the potatoes and fungi. The poached egg perched on top isn't just for show. As you break the yolk, it bleeds into the hot liquid, thickening it further and providing a satisfying boost of protein. 

I always add a slice of dark, dense rye bread on the side. The crusty edges are perfect for mopping up any lingering drops of the creamy, dill-flecked broth. If you want a slightly more substantial lunch, a simple side salad of peppery rocket and shaved radishes cuts through the richness of the egg yolk beautifully.

The trick I learnt the hard way about storing this soup is to keep the poached eggs separate. The soup itself keeps wonderfully in the fridge for up to three days, and the mushroom flavour actually deepens overnight. When you are ready to eat, reheat the liquid gently on the stove and poach a fresh egg at the last minute. Freezing is best avoided, as the potatoes tend to take on an unpleasantly grainy texture when thawed.
