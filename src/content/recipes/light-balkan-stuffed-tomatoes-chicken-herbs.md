steps:
  - "Preheat your oven to 180°C (350°F)."
  - "Slice the tops off the tomatoes and set them aside; these will be your 'lids'. Carefully scoop out the pulp and seeds with a spoon, leaving a sturdy shell. Finely chop the scooped-out pulp and reserve it."
  - "Lightly salt the inside of the tomato shells and turn them upside down on a wire rack or paper towel for about 10 minutes. This helps drain excess moisture."
  - "While the tomatoes drain, heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and listen for that gentle sizzle. Sauté until softened, about 5 minutes."
  - "Add the minced garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant, then add the lean ground chicken. Cook, breaking it up with a spoon, until it's no longer pink."
  - "Stir in the cooked rice, the reserved chopped tomato pulp, fresh parsley, fresh dill, and paprika. Season with salt and black pepper and cook for 2-3 minutes, allowing all the flavours to meld together."
  - "Pat the inside of the tomato shells dry. Arrange them snugly in a baking dish. Carefully spoon the chicken and rice mixture into each tomato, packing it in gently."
  - "Place the reserved tomato tops back on each tomato. Pour the chicken or vegetable broth into the bottom of the baking dish, around the tomatoes."
  - "Bake for 25-30 minutes. You'll know they're ready when the tomatoes are tender and the filling is piping hot. Let them rest for a few minutes before you serve them."

Stuffed vegetables are the heart and soul of summer cooking across the Balkans and Eastern Europe. My mum made stuffed peppers every August, a rich and hearty version with pork mince that would perfume the entire house. It was a signal that the harvest was in full swing. While I adore that nostalgic meal, it can feel a bit heavy for a regular weeknight. This recipe is my lighter take on that tradition, keeping all the flavour but making it something you can feel good about eating any day.

I've swapped the traditional pork or beef for lean ground chicken and packed the filling with fresh summer herbs like parsley and dill. The result is a dish that’s bright, fragrant, and surprisingly filling without weighing you down. The real magic happens in the oven, when the tomatoes soften and release their sweet juices into the savoury filling. The amazing smell of baked tomatoes and herbs filling the kitchen is pure comfort to me, a perfect blend of old-world tradition and modern, healthy eating.

This dish is proof that you don't need a lot of fat or heavy ingredients to create something deeply satisfying. It's a celebration of simple, seasonal ingredients, where the sweetness of the sun-ripened tomatoes and the freshness of the herbs do all the work. It’s a complete, balanced meal that’s both beautiful on the plate and great for your body.

### How to Serve Your Stuffed Tomatoes

These tomatoes are fantastic on their own, as they have protein, carbs, and vegetables all in one neat package. I always serve them with a big dollop of thick, plain Greek yogurt on the side. The cool, tangy yogurt is the perfect contrast to the warm, savoury filling. A simple side salad of cucumbers, red onion, and a light vinaigrette also works beautifully to complete the meal.

### Variations and Storing Leftovers

Don't be afraid to make this recipe your own! Ground turkey works just as well as chicken. If you want to add more vegetables, some finely chopped zucchini or mushrooms sautéed with the onion would be a great addition. For a nuttier flavour and more fibre, you can easily substitute the rice with cooked quinoa.

Leftovers are a delight. Store any extra stuffed tomatoes in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. They reheat perfectly in the microwave for a quick lunch, or you can pop them back in a moderate oven for 10-15 minutes until heated through.
