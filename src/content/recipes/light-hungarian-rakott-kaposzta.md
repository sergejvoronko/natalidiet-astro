Growing up close to the Hungarian border, the smell of braised cabbage and paprika was a constant backdrop to autumn evenings. Traditional rakott káposzta is a heavy, comforting mountain of pork, smoked sausages, and thick layers of full-fat sour cream. It tastes brilliant, but it leaves you wanting to sleep for the rest of the afternoon. This version keeps that deep, comforting flavour without the heaviness.

Swapping fatty minced pork for lean ground turkey cuts the calorie count right down while keeping the protein high. The trick is getting the seasoning right. Since we are leaving out the traditional smoked kolbász, I add a generous spoonful of smoked paprika alongside the classic sweet Hungarian paprika. The moment those red spices hit the warm olive oil in the skillet, the kitchen smells incredible, earthy, sweet, and smoky all at once. Brown rice steps in for the usual white rice, adding a subtle chew and keeping you fuller for longer thanks to the extra fibre.

Serving this casserole is straightforward. It holds its shape best if you let it rest for about ten minutes after pulling it from the oven, allowing the bubbling juices to settle. I like to plate it up with a scattering of fresh dill or parsley across the top, and maybe some pickled mild peppers on the side for a vinegary crunch that cuts through the rich sour cream topping. 

If you end up with leftovers, you are in luck. Layered cabbage dishes almost always taste better the next day once the juices have thoroughly soaked into the rice and turkey. Store it in an airtight container in the fridge for up to four days, and reheat individual portions in the microwave or a hot oven. You can easily adapt the layers, too. Bulgur wheat makes a fantastic, slightly nuttier substitute for the brown rice, or you can swap the turkey for a mix of brown lentils and mushrooms for a completely meat-free variation.
