I grew up eating a variation of this hearty dish almost every New Year's Day, as lentils are meant to symbolise coins and bring prosperity for the months ahead. This Czech lentil stew, traditionally known as čočka na kyselo, translates literally to sour lentils. It sounds humble, but the moment the sharp vinegar hits the earthy, slow-cooked legumes, the whole pot wakes up. I still remember standing by the stove listening to the quiet, rhythmic blip of the thick stew simmering, the smell of toasted onions and pungent marjoram filling our tiny kitchen.

Traditionally, this cocka na kyselo recipe relies on a heavy roux made with lots of lard and flour to achieve its signature creamy texture. I prefer to make it much lighter for a healthy Eastern European lunch. By relying mostly on the natural starches of the lentils as they break down, I only need a tiny pinch of flour to get that comforting, thick consistency. The result is just as satisfying but sits much better in the stomach, leaving you energised rather than sleepy.

Serving this dish with a runny poached egg is non-negotiable for me. When you break the yolk, it runs down into the tangy brown lentils, creating a rich sauce that balances the vinegar's bright bite perfectly. It is a fantastic vegetarian meal packed with plant protein and fibre.

### Serving Ideas

My favourite way to eat this is scooped up with a thick slice of dense rye sourdough. The crusty bread is brilliant for wiping the bowl clean of every last drop of yolk and tangy broth. If I'm making this for a larger weekend lunch, I sometimes throw together a sharp, simple cucumber salad on the side to cut through the richness of the egg. For the traditionalists out there, you can swap the poached egg for a slice of lean smoked ham or a roasted frankfurter, though I find the vegetarian version feels much cleaner.

### Storage and Variations

The stew itself tastes even better the next day after the flavours have mingled in the fridge overnight. Just store the lentils in an airtight container for up to four days and gently reheat them on the stove with a splash of water to loosen the sauce. You should always make your eggs fresh, as reheated poached eggs turn into rubbery little bouncy balls. If you don't have brown lentils, French green Puy lentils work beautifully and hold their shape even better, though they'll need an extra ten minutes of simmering time. You can also play around with the acidity, swapping white vinegar for apple cider vinegar or a squeeze of fresh lemon juice if you prefer a fruitier finish.
