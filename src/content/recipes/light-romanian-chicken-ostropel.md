Ostropel de pui translates roughly to chicken ostropel, a traditional Romanian stew defined by an unapologetic amount of garlic. It relies on a thick, vibrant tomato sauce that coats the chicken and carries the intense flavours. I prefer making it with skinless chicken breast instead of the traditional bone-in, skin-on pieces to create a lighter, high-protein meal. The sharp, savoury aroma of garlic hitting the warm oil fills the kitchen instantly, setting the stage for a comforting dish you can explore further in my guide to [Healthy Romanian Recipes](/blog/healthy-romanian-recipes/).

The acidity from the crushed tomatoes and a subtle splash of white wine vinegar balances the savoury depth. Garlic is the star here, and the trick is to layer it. I cook half of the garlic early on to sweeten the sauce, and stir the rest in during the final minutes so it retains its raw, spicy bite. You get the full traditional Romanian flavour profile without the heavy layer of oil usually left in the pan.

Serving this stew is straightforward. The rich tomato and garlic sauce needs something to cling to, making a slice of crusty bread or a portion of mămăligă ideal. You can find a lighter version of that classic side in my [Light Romanian Mămăligă with Cottage Cheese & Kefir](/recipes/light-romanian-mamaliga-cottage-cheese-kefir/). If you want a fresh, crisp contrast, pair the ostropel with a simple [Light Romanian Corn & Bell Pepper Salad](/recipes/light-romanian-corn-bell-pepper-salad/).

Leftovers store brilliantly in the fridge for up to four days in an airtight container. The garlic actually mellows and infuses the tomato sauce more overnight, making the second day even better. Reheat gently on the stove over low heat to prevent the lean chicken breast from drying out. You can also freeze the stew in a sealed container for up to three months.

If you want a slight variation, skinless chicken thighs work beautifully and remain very tender, though they adjust the fat content slightly. Some cooks like to stir a spoonful of yogurt or light sour cream into the sauce just before serving, taking the edge off the acidity and adding a creamy finish. You can also toss in a handful of diced bell peppers alongside the tomatoes for extra vegetables.
