steps:
  - "Place the dried wild mushrooms in a small bowl, cover with boiling water, and leave them to soak for 15 minutes."
  - "Combine the cooled, riced potatoes and roasted pumpkin puree in a large mixing bowl."
  - "Add the egg, 120g of all-purpose flour, and salt to the bowl, kneading gently just until a soft dough forms."
  - "Transfer the dough to a floured surface, divide it into four sections, and roll each into a long rope about 2 centimeters thick."
  - "Cut each rope diagonally into small, diamond-shaped dumplings and set them aside on a lightly floured board."
  - "Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium heat, then add the diced onion and cook until translucent, about 4 minutes."
  - "Drain the soaked wild mushrooms, reserving the soaking liquid, and chop them roughly before adding them to the skillet along with the fresh sliced cremini mushrooms and minced garlic."
  - "Cook the mushrooms until they release their moisture and brown slightly, then pour in the vegetable broth and the reserved mushroom soaking liquid."
  - "Simmer the sauce for 5 to 7 minutes until slightly reduced, then stir in the sour cream, salt, and black pepper, turning off the heat immediately so the cream doesn't curdle."
  - "Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil and drop the kopytka in batches, cooking for about 2 minutes after they float to the surface."
  - "Remove the dumplings with a slotted spoon, toss them directly into the warm mushroom sauce, and garnish with fresh chopped parsley before serving."

Traditional Polish kopytka, which translates to "little hooves" thanks to their diagonal cut, are dense, comforting potato dumplings. By blending roasted pumpkin into the potato base, these dumplings become a vibrant autumn dish that feels lighter on the stomach. The pumpkin adds a subtle natural sweetness and a beautiful orange hue, while the earthy forest mushroom sauce brings deep, savoury umami. The sharp sizzle of onions hitting the hot skillet and the rich scent of rehydrating wild mushrooms immediately make the kitchen feel warm and inviting. 

Creating <a href="/blog/healthy-polish-recipes/">Healthy Polish Recipes</a> often means making small adjustments to traditional methods. Instead of relying heavily on butter and thick double cream for the sauce, I use a combination of aromatic vegetable broth and just a small dollop of light sour cream. This keeps the fat content low but maintains the silky texture that clings to the warm dumplings. The dried porcini mushrooms do the heavy lifting here, infusing the simple broth with an intense, woodsy flavour that fresh mushrooms alone can't provide.

A plate of soft dumplings and earthy sauce needs a bit of acidity to balance the palate. I like to serve these alongside a sharp, crisp side dish like fermented cabbage or a quick cucumber salad tossed in apple cider vinegar and fresh dill. The crunch of raw vegetables offers a great textural contrast to the pillowy kopytka. If you want to boost the protein further, a side of grilled chicken or white fish works perfectly with the mushroom gravy.

Freezing raw kopytka is incredibly practical for busy weeknights. arrange the uncooked, cut dumplings on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, making sure they don't touch. Once they freeze solid, transfer them to a freezer-safe bag. When dinner time rolls around, you can drop the frozen dumplings directly into boiling salted water, just adding an extra minute or two to the cooking time until they float to the top.

The dough is quite forgiving, allowing for a few subtle tweaks. A pinch of ground nutmeg or white pepper kneaded directly into the potato and pumpkin mixture highlights the squash's natural sweetness. If you prefer a completely dairy-free sauce, swap the light sour cream for an unsweetened oat or soy alternative, making sure to temper it with a little hot broth before stirring it into the pan to prevent splitting.
